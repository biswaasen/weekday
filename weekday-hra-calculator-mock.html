How HRA exemption is calculated

Your tax-free HRA is the lowest of three numbers. Everything above that is taxed.

1

Actual HRA

The HRA your company pays you, as shown on your payslip.
₹25,000 × 12 = ₹3,00,000

2

Rent − 10% of basic

Rent you pay in a year, minus 10% of your yearly basic + DA.
₹2,64,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹2,04,000

3

50% or 40% of basic

50% of basic + DA in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai. 40% in every other city.
50% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹3,00,000

Frequently asked questions

How is HRA exemption calculated?

It is the lowest of three: the HRA you actually get, rent paid minus 10% of basic + DA, and 50% of basic + DA (40% outside Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai).

Can I claim HRA in the new tax regime?

No. HRA exemption is only available in the old regime. If your rent is high, check whether the old regime gives you more in-hand with the in-hand salary calculator.

Is Bengaluru a metro city for HRA?

Not for HRA. Only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai get the 50% limit. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and other cities use 40%.

Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents?

Yes, if you actually pay them, keep rent receipts or bank transfers, and they show the rent as income in their tax return. You cannot pay rent to your spouse and claim HRA.

Do I need my landlord's PAN?

Yes, if the rent you pay is more than ₹1 lakh a year. Below that, rent receipts are enough.

What if I do not get HRA from my company?

You cannot claim HRA exemption, but you may be able to claim a rent deduction under Section 80GG in the old regime, if you meet its conditions.
